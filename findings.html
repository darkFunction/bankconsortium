How much private interbank payment traffic can Paladin and Besu carry?

The use case: one bank pays another in private cash tokens, with a private side effect such as a bond transfer, and no batching of payments. The DvP tab in this demo is the maximalist version, with a regulator on the critical path. This page records what we measured, and what that implies for a network of about 20 banks.

Paladin v0.15.0 and Besu 25.8.0 (QBFT) on one 16-core / 30 GiB laptop. Tags: measured observed in these runs; estimate derived from measured costs plus a stated assumption.

The short answer

Is Besu capable of a national payments network?

YesWholesale interbank settlement (RTGS-style volumes of a few to ~10 payments/s): one chain carried about 90–115 private payments/s.
NoRetail instant payments (hundreds to thousands per second): one chain tops out at a fixed ~100–115 Mgas/s, so this needs many chains.

Is Paladin capable of a national payments network?

Not yetWholesale volume is within reach: ~7 payments/s at 32 bank pairs, and it scales with banks.
BlockerMaturity: v0.15 failed 3.5% of payments and retries didn't fix them, and Zeto proves one payment at a time per bank per token. Those are product problems, not limits of the design.

What was tested

  • The Paladin cluster behind this demo: 4 Paladin nodes and 4 Besu QBFT validators in kind, 700M gas limit, blocks effectively every ~1 s (see Latency).
  • Closed-loop concurrency sweeps of the DvP, of each building block (Noto, Zeto, Pente), and of the recommended payment design. Each measured steady-state throughput, latency percentiles, gas, and per-container CPU.
  • A separate saturation test of the chain alone: a throwaway Besu network flooded with synthetic transactions matched on gas and cryptographic work, because a laptop's Paladin nodes can't generate enough load to find the chain's limit.

The maximalist DvP peaks at 0.7 trades/s

The DvP needs 8 base-ledger transactions and 2.76M gas per trade, with an Atom contract deployed per trade. It peaked at 0.7 DvP/s with 4–6 trades in flight; beyond that latency went from 5.5 s to 18.5 s and throughput fell. measured

Hardware isn't the limit: the whole cluster used about 2 of 16 cores at the peak. The limit is the single shared oversight Pente group that every bond operation passes through, together with its coordinating node's Postgres. Concurrent transactions race for that group's state, and beyond about 8 in flight its coordinator thrashes.

LessonKeep shared groups, such as a regulator's oversight group, off the critical path of each payment. Give oversight asynchronously, or through per-pair groups.

Recommended design: private payment with an atomic side effect

This is the smallest transaction graph that keeps the cash leg and the side effect atomic without a shared lane: no Atom deployment, and each bank pair only ever contends with itself. It is built and shown in the “Private payment + side effect (2 rounds)” tab.

round 1 (parallel):  Bank B: Zeto lock(amount, delegate = the A⇄B Pente group)   base tx  346k gas
                     Bank B: group.bid(tradeId, units)                          base tx  146k gas
then:                Bank B: prepare the locked transfer to Bank A (ZK proof, off-chain, ~0.2 s)
round 2:             Bank A: group.settle(tradeId, units, cash transfer)        base tx  457k gas
                       └─ bond A→B in the private register + Zeto.transferLocked,
                          in ONE base-ledger transaction

Only Bank A and Bank B can read the bond register or value the cash. Bank C, a consortium member with a node on the same chain, is denied the group and sees every balance as 0.

Before production 3.5% of payments (22 of 620) failed on Paladin v0.15 state races, and retries never rescued one. A failed payment leaves the buyer's cash locked to the group because this proof-of-concept contract has no refund path. The seller also needs an off-chain check of the prepared cash before calling settle; the contract can't make it because amounts are hidden from it too.

If the cash issuer may see interbank flows, the same design with Noto (own-liability deposits) instead of Zeto removes proving entirely, at the cost of the issuer seeing amounts.

One chain, saturated: a fixed ~100–115 Mgas/s

  • Capacity is a gas rate, the same for every transaction type and configuration. Besu executes at about 300 Mgas/s while building a block, but building, importing and persisting stretch each saturated block cycle to about 1.8 s.
  • Parallel execution gave nothing, even though every transaction wrote separate storage, which is its best case.

  • Gas limit isn't the constraint: 700M gas per block would allow ~820 payments/s, but execution time binds first.

Scaling to 20 banks, and better hardware

Network throughput is the smallest of three limits: the sum of every bank's send capacity, the capacity of each bank pair's lane, and the shared chain's execution rate. The first two grow as banks join. The third is fixed, because every node re-executes every transaction. estimate

LimitRecommended design, Zeto cashHow it scales
Per bank~3.5 payments/s per cash token; ~20/s with tokens sharded across 16 coresLinearly with banks and cores
20 banks combined~70/s with one token each → ~400/s shardedLinearly
Per bank pair≤ ~10/s, keeping ≤ ~8 in flightOnly binds on hot pairs
The shared chain~90–115/s measured on this laptop; perhaps 1.5–2× on fast serversFixed: doesn't grow with banks
  • Twenty banks aren't 5× faster than four. They add private capacity, but the shared chain becomes the limit.
  • More validators don't add capacity. Keep 4–7 validators and run the other banks' Besu nodes as followers; every follower still has to keep up with block import.
  • Better hardware raises bank-side capacity almost linearly, but the chain only by single-thread speed: about 1.5–2× from a laptop to a fast server.

  • On one chain, only plain Noto transfers come close, and only if server CPUs deliver the remaining ~2×. That's plausible but unmeasured.
  • Otherwise it takes several chains: roughly 10–18 for the Zeto design, or 5–9 with Noto own-liability. Banks are partitioned by currency, region or business line, and atomic settlement only works within a chain.
  • Zeto has a second wall: about 1,500 cores of proving across the network, roughly 75 per bank with 20 banks. Noto own-liability removes it, so whether issuers may see interbank flows is the decision that matters most.
  • Proof-level batching (one proof and transaction covering several outputs from the same payer) is the only lever that cuts both walls. It's untested, and settlement batching is ruled out for this use case.

Latency: the 1 s per step is a configuration artefact

Besu does apply the cluster's 100 ms block period. But the genesis also sets a 10 s empty-block period (xemptyblockperiodseconds). Whenever the transaction pool is momentarily empty, which Paladin's request-then-wait traffic causes all the time, Besu falls back to checking once a second, so each step waits up to ~1.1 s. The saturation network reproduced that cadence exactly; without the empty-block period, transactions were included in about 0.3 s. measured

That would cut the recommended payment from 2.2 s to well under a second. It doesn't change throughput, and it needs a genesis change on every node, which hasn't been made to the demo cluster.

Caveats

  • One laptop. All nodes shared one machine and an untuned sidecar Postgres per node. Node-side numbers are floors; re-run the chain test on the hardware you would deploy.
  • Synthetic chain load. The saturation transactions match the real ones on gas and cryptographic work but aren't the real contracts. Real transactions can't be replayed at volume, because each spends unique states.
  • Short runs of roughly 10–60 s per level. Sustained runs haven't been done, so “sustainable” isn't established.
  • Paladin v0.15 races needed client retries at moderate concurrency; reliability under load is itself a finding. A newer Paladin may behave differently, untested.
  • Serial Zeto proving per node and token is an inference from the measurements, not verified in Paladin's code.
  • The recommended design is a proof of concept: no refund path, no seller-side check of the prepared cash, and a toy bond register.
